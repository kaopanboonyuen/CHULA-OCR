Research motivation

OCR for legal cadastral documents remains extremely challenging under severe real-world degradation.

Real-world land title deeds are noisy, degraded, and legally sensitive.

Existing OCR systems fail under handwriting overlays, faded scans, official stamps, blur, and layout corruption.

Even minor recognition errors can produce critical legal inconsistencies in document digitization systems.

CHULA-OCR introduces uncertainty-aware perception for robust document intelligence under real operational constraints.

1Part 1 of 2: Object detection

Why detection on Thai land title deeds is extraordinarily hard

Thai cadastral documents are one of the most hostile environments for computer vision. Boundary markers (survey pins), land parcel annotations, official stamps, faded ink, handwritten overlays, and dense spatial clutter create a detection challenge unlike anything in standard document AI benchmarks. Our CHULA model tackles this head-on, detecting every pin, boundary segment, and cadastral object under real operational conditions.

2Part 2 of 2: OCR extraction

From detected pin to digital text

Once cadastral objects are detected, CHULA-OCR reads the handwritten and printed alphanumeric labels attached to each survey pin. These labels are unique parcel identifiers and must be read with near-perfect accuracy to feed downstream land registry systems. The challenge is severe: blurred crops, mixed Thai numerals, inconsistent inking, and extreme variation in handwriting across decades of historical documents.

Built for production, not just the lab

CHULA-OCR is engineered as a production-ready AI system for Thailand's Department of Lands. By digitizing pin labels at scale, it enables searchable, verifiable, and legally auditable land records, turning decades of paper archives into a living national cadastral database.
