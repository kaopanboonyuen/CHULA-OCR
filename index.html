Research Motivation

OCR for legal cadastral documents remains extremely challenging under severe real-world degradation.

Real-world land title deeds are noisy, degraded, and legally sensitive.

Existing OCR systems fail under handwriting overlays, faded scans, official stamps, blur, and layout corruption.

Even minor recognition errors can produce critical legal inconsistencies in document digitization systems.

CHULA-OCR introduces uncertainty-aware perception for robust document intelligence under real operational constraints.

Core Methodology

Robust OCR through uncertainty-aware structured perception.

Uncertainty Estimation

Detect unreliable visual regions under severe degradation, handwriting contamination, and scanning artifacts.

Feature Gating

Suppress noisy document patterns including blur, occlusion, official stamps, and structural corruption.

Structured Decoding

Preserve cadastral formatting consistency for reliable downstream legal verification.

🔍 Part 1 of 2 · Object Detection

Why Detection on Thai Land
Title Deeds Is Extraordinarily Hard

Thai cadastral documents present one of the most hostile environments for computer vision. Boundary markers (survey pins), land parcel annotations, official stamps, faded ink, handwritten overlays, and dense spatial clutter create a detection challenge unlike anything seen in standard document AI benchmarks. Our CHULA model tackles this head-on — detecting every pin, boundary segment, and cadastral object under real operational conditions.

🧠 Part 2 of 2 · OCR Extraction

From Detected Pin to Digital Text —
CHULA-OCR in Action

Once cadastral objects are detected, CHULA-OCR extracts the handwritten and printed alphanumeric labels attached to each survey pin. These labels — unique parcel identifiers — must be read with near-perfect accuracy to feed into downstream land registry systems. The OCR challenge is severe: blurred crops, mixed Thai numerals, inconsistent inking, and extreme variation in handwriting style across decades of historical documents.

🇹🇭 Impact

CHULA-OCR is engineered not as a research curiosity, but as a production-ready AI system for Thailand's Department of Lands. By digitizing pin labels at scale, this system enables searchable, verifiable, and legally auditable land records — transforming decades of paper archives into a living national cadastral database.
